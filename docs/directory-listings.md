### G2 — do this one first
- Claim or add: `https://www.g2.com/seller_identification/search/new`
- Vendor portal afterwards: `https://my.g2.com`
- Free tier is permanent: profile, screenshots, video, pricing, integrations, 3 users.
- The product does **not** need to already exist in G2's index — the same page has
  an "add your product" path, and their research team creates and categorises it.
- **Email domain must match the product website.** This is the single most common
  rejection — it is exactly why support@vrikso.com had to exist before starting.
- Timing: claim ~1–3 business days, new product ~3–5 business days.
- Reality check on reviews: a profile can live with zero reviews, but **10 reviews
  in a category** is the threshold to appear on a G2 Grid. Reviews must be from real
  users within the last 2 years; employees and competitors are filtered out.

### Capterra / GetApp / Software Advice — one submission, three sites
- **G2 acquired these from Gartner** (announced Jan 2026, closed Feb 2026). The old
  vendor portals `vendors.capterra.com` and `digitalmarkets.gartner.com` no longer
  resolve. Any guide pointing there is stale.
- Get listed: `https://app.g2digitalmarkets.com/get-listed/start`
- The "Launch" plan is **$0**. Paid tiers are pay-per-click/lead — skip them for now.
- One profile feeds all three sites and they share a single review pool, so a review
  collected once counts three times.
- They **do not accept pre-launch products** — the software must be live and
  purchasable. Vrikso qualifies.
- Expect some portal friction; the platforms are still merging.

## 9. Google Play Store — needs an Android build and a D-U-N-S number

Verified 2026-11-27 against `~/My-business-crm`: there is **no Android project,
no Capacitor, no Cordova, no TWA wrapper**. So "create a Play Store listing" is not
a form-filling task like the directories — it needs a shippable Android artifact first.

The pragmatic path is a **TWA (Trusted Web Activity)** — a thin Android shell around
the existing PWA at app.vrikso.com, generated by Bubblewrap or PWABuilder. The app is
already most of the way there: it serves a web manifest (`application/manifest+json`),
registers a service worker, and is HTTPS. Note the marketing site (`vrikso-site`) has
**no** manifest or service worker — that's correct and irrelevant here, because the
thing to wrap is the product, not the brochure.

### Step 1 — the D-U-N-S application (route traced 2026-11-27)

**Exact URL for India: `https://www.dnb.co.in/duns/get-a-duns`**

How I got there, in case the link rots: dnb.com/duns/get-a-duns.html → "I have an
International-based business" → "I'm a Google developer" → Asia Pacific tab → India.
India is a D&B *owned* market (not a network partner), so it has its own portal.

- **Cost: free.** Standard processing **up to 30 business days**. Expedited (~5 days) is
  free for US FDA and Apple Developer applicants but **chargeable for Google Developer**.
- On the form, set **Primary reason for registration = "Google Developer"**.
- Support: the DUNS contact address on dnb.co.in.

**Form fields** (no document uploads at submission):
legal name · headquarters name and address · DBA name · physical address, city, state,
PIN · mailing address if different · telephone · contact name and title · number of
employees at that location · home-based business yes/no · state · domestic turnover
range · query type · product name · primary reason · **GSTIN (optional on the form)** ·
marketing and data-cloud consent checkboxes.

**Documents — not uploaded, but demanded later.** D&B verifies by contacting you, and
per D&B India's own guidance will ask for: **PAN card, GST certificate, Certificate of
Incorporation or other registration proof** (partnership deed / proprietorship
declaration for non-companies)**, address proof, and authorised signatory details.**
Have these ready; the clock restarts if you're slow to respond.

**Check before applying:** search `service.dnb.com` for the legal entity first. Many
already-registered Indian companies have a D-U-N-S number, and finding an existing one
skips the entire 30-day wait.

### Register as an organization, not a personal account

Personal developer accounts created after 13 Nov 2023 must run a closed test with
**12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days** before they can publish to
production. This rule is still in force. **Organization accounts are exempt** and can
publish straight to production — but they require a **D-U-N-S number**, matching
organization name and address, and Google re-checks that match on an ongoing basis.

So the trade is: ~30 days waiting for a free D-U-N-S number, versus recruiting 12 real
testers and keeping them opted in for 14 unbroken days. For a registered company the
organization route is lower risk — the tester requirement is what strands most solo
developers for months.

### Other hard requirements

- **Target API 36 (Android 16)** — mandatory for new apps submitted after 31 Aug 2026.
  Use a current Bubblewrap release; a stale one generates a build Play will reject.
- **Bubblewrap needs JDK 17 exactly** — higher versions are incompatible with the
  Android command-line tools. PWABuilder (pwabuilder.com) is the no-CLI alternative.
- **Wrap `app.vrikso.com`, not `vrikso.com`.** A wrapper around a marketing site fails
  Play's minimum-functionality policy. The logged-in product passes it.

### The assetlinks trap

`assetlinks.json` must contain the SHA-256 fingerprint of the **Play-managed app
signing key**, not your local upload key — and that fingerprint only exists *after*
the first upload to Play Console. So the order is: create the app → upload the AAB →
copy the signing fingerprint out of Play Console → publish assetlinks.json → verify.

It must return **200, `Content-Type: application/json`, with no redirect**. Android's
verifier rejects 301/302 outright. (This is why the middleware fix above mattered —
the path was 307-ing to /login.)

**Do not ship the TWA before assetlinks verifies.** If it fails, the app opens with a
browser address bar visible — which both looks broken and is itself a rejection
trigger under the minimum-functionality policy.

## 10. Techjockey — a decision, not a form ⚠️

Techjockey is **not** a free directory listing like the others. I pulled their actual
Service Agreement (`cdn.techjockey.com/eseller/docs/Service_Agreement.pdf`). Listing is
free of charge, but the terms are a reseller relationship:

- **Commission is negotiated per brand before listing** (clause 5.11.f) — which is why
  there is no self-serve path to live. Someone from their alliance team has to agree a
  margin with you first.
- **Techjockey owns the customer relationship** (clause 5.11). All commercial and
  technical communication with the buyer runs through their sales team, purchases go
  through techjockey.com, and **you may not quote the customer directly.**
- **Penalty for direct contact** (clause 5.9): contacting a Techjockey-sourced customer
  directly for renewal or expansion costs **5× the commission or 2× gross transaction
  value, whichever is higher**, plus a security cheque within 7 days.
- They pay you within 45 days of delivery. Either side can terminate on 30 days notice.
- Registration needs **GSTIN, PAN, bank details, product HSN and service SAC**.

Entry point for the commission conversation: `alliance@techjockey.com`, or sign up at
`esellerhub.techjockey.com`.

**This needs a decision, not just a form fill.** For a ₹349–999/month product where the
whole strategy is self-serve published pricing and direct WhatsApp contact, handing the
customer relationship to a reseller — with a 5× penalty for talking to your own
customer — cuts against the positioning. The SEO/AI-citation benefit of the listing is
real, but so is the lock-in. Worth deciding deliberately before opening the negotiation.
